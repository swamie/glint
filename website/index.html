IReplay buffer

A buffer that is always a key away.

The buffer runs while you play and holds up to an hour. Each clip key has its own length, so one key keeps thirty seconds and another keeps ten minutes of the same buffer.

  • Kept in RAM, on disk, or both
  • A key per length, and a monitor per key
  • A sound and a small overlay when a clip is saved

IITimeline editor

An editor built for cutting clips.

Split, cut out and trim on a timeline with the clip's own sound on separate rows. Add a song and it plays straight through every cut, so the beat never jumps when the footage does.

  • Game, desktop and microphone on their own rows
  • Several clips in one project, in any order
  • Speed, color looks, fades, and undo for all of it

IIICanvas

Vertical video from a wide clip.

Pick 9:16 and place pieces of the picture on the canvas: the gameplay in the middle, the kill feed zoomed above it, the health bar below. Save the layout and the next clip takes one click.

  • 9:16, 1:1, 4:5, or a plain crop at its own size
  • Drag to move, a corner scales, an edge crops
  • The clip blurred, black or a color behind the pieces

IVOverlays and keyframes

Text, shapes and blur that follow the action.

Type on the picture, draw on it, or drag a box that blurs what is inside. Add a keyframe, move the playhead, drag the item, and it travels between the two.

  • Text, rectangles, arrows, freehand and blur boxes
  • Keyframes for position and size, linear or smooth
  • Each item on screen for the time you choose
clutch

VCapture and sound

The game alone, or the whole desktop.

One switch decides what is recorded. GAME takes only the game's window and its own sound. DESKTOP takes the monitor and everything you hear. A mixer sets how loud each source is in your clips.

  • A fader and a live meter for desktop, microphone and game
  • Pick the output device and the microphone
  • Noise suppression for your voice when a clip is saved

VIRender rules

One button for the whole export.

Chain the steps you repeat for every clip into a rule: a color look, a saved vertical layout, motion blur, a file size. Run it from the right-click menu and walk away.

  • Compress to a size, such as 50 MB
  • GIF export and upscaling to 4K
  • Motion blur through Blur, rendered in a queue

VIIIQuestions

Short answers before you install.

Does Glint upload anything?

No. Clips are files in a folder you choose. There is no account and no server. Once a day Glint asks GitHub whether a newer version exists, and you can switch that off.

What does it need?

Windows 10 or 11 and ffmpeg 8 or newer. Glint finds ffmpeg by itself and offers to fetch the pieces it is missing. A graphics card with a hardware encoder keeps the load low while you play.

How do updates work?

Glint installs a new version by itself while its window is closed and no game is running, then starts again in the tray with clipping as it was.

Can I bring my old clips?

Yes. The import dialog reads the folders of other clipping software and adds those clips to your library, in place or as copies.

What languages are currently supported?

English, Korean and Greek.
